Personal Branding...

Why is it important?



Why is Personal Branding Important?



  • Helps you stand out in a competitive job market

  • Shows potential employers who you are beyond your résumé

  • Keeps your look consistent across platforms so people are more likely to remember you

What is Personal Branding?



  • How you present yourself professionally

  • Tell me a story: about your skills, values, and passions

  • Grow and evolve with your experience

Where to Show Your Brand



  • Portfolio website

  • LinkedIn profile

  • GitHub profile

  • Networking events and online communities

Finding Your Brand in 3 Steps



  1. Discover: answer a few questions about yourself

  2. Be consistent: choose a look and a voice

  3. Apply: put it everywhere people will find you

🤔 Self-Discovery Questions



  • What are your top strengths? (problem-solving, creativity, communication)

  • What are you most passionate about, inside and outside tech?

  • What best describes your personality?

  • What are your professional goals?

Make It Consistent,


  • Which colors, fonts, or visual styles feel most "you"?

  • 3 to 5 words that describe your tone (friendly, bold, analytical)

  • One headshot or logo everywhere

  • One storytelling voice

Then Apply it


  • Draft a short "about me" blurb

  • List the projects you've done or are doing

  • Add your headshot or logo to every platform

  • Optional: choose a phrase or mantra that reflects your mission

What Goes in a Portfolio?



👀 Every Visitor Is Asking Three Questions



  1. Who are you?

  2. What can you do?

  3. How do I reach you?

The Core Sections


  • Home: your name and a tagline

  • About: background, passions, and goals

  • Projects: links, screenshots, and short descriptions

  • Contact: email and links to your profiles

  • Bonus: résumé download

  • Beginner tip: one scrolling page is plenty. You'll build a single index.html tonight.

The Top of Your Homepage


  • Your name

  • A one-line tagline

  • A clear next step, like a View my work button

  • Optional: a photo or logo

🏷️ Tagline Formula


I'm [name], a [role] who [what you do or who you help].


I'm Perla, a front-end web developer who works with small businesses to create user-friendly websites.


Writing Your About Section


  1. Hook: who you are in one sentence

  2. Story: how you got here, in 2 to 3 sentences

  3. Skills: the tools you use

  4. Beyond the code: what you love outside of tech

  5. Invitation: "Let's connect"

🔄 Switching Careers?


Your last career is an asset



Example: "Ten years of teaching taught me to explain complex ideas simply, and that's what I bring to every interface I build."

📬 Contact


  • Email, LinkedIn, and GitHub links

  • Simplest option, a mailto link:
    • <a href="mailto:you@example.com">Email me</a>

  • A contact form needs a form service, because a static site can't process forms on its own

🚫 What to Leave Out



  • Home address or personal phone number

  • "Coming soon" pages and placeholder text

  • Broken links

📁 Your Site Is Made of Three Files


  • index.html:
    Holds the structure and content

  • style.css:
    Controls the appearance; colors, fonts, layout

  • script.js:
    Controls the behavior; interactivity

Showcasing Projects


Quality over Quantity



  • 2 to 4 strong projects beat 10 thin ones

  • Put your best or most relevant project first

  • Done and polished wins over ambitious and abandoned.

Anatomy of a Project Card


  • A screenshot with descriptive alt text

  • A title

  • A one-line description

  • The tools you used

  • Links to the live site or the code

Turn Your Best Project into a Mini Case Study



  1. Problem: what were you trying to solve?

  2. Approach: what did you build, and with what?

  3. Result: what happened?

  4. What I learned: one honest takeaway

🌱 "I Don't Have Projects Yet"



  • This portfolio is project number one

  • Worksheets and exercises from the Coding & Cocktails series

  • A redesign of a local business or nonprofit page

  • Volunteer or class projects

🧹 GitHub Housekeeping



  • Give each project a README: what it is, a link to the live site, the tools used, a screenshot, and what you learned

  • Pin your best repositories to your GitHub profile

  • Add your live site link to each repository's About section

Building It


📐 Why Plan First?



  • Five minutes of sketching can save an hour of rework

  • Make your layout, color, and type decisions once

  • Builders draw a blueprint before they pick up a hammer

What Is a Wireframe?


  • A simple, low-detail layout: boxes and labels

  • No color, no real photos, no polish

  • Shows what goes where, and in what order

Wireframe Example


✏️ Wireframing Tips


  • Start with a phone-sized, single-column sketch, then imagine it wider

  • Label every box

  • Bigger box means more important

  • Leave empty space on purpose

  • Paper and pencil are perfect tonight. Figma's free plan is a good digital option for later.

🎨 Choosing Colors


  • Start with one color you already love, such as one from your logo

  • Write every color as a hex code, like #E4572E

  • Pick 1 background, 1 text color, and 1 or 2 accent colors

  • A common guideline: about 60% background, 30% secondary, 10% accent

Tools


  • Magic Pattern color palette generator: magicpattern.design

  • mycolor.space: mycolor.space

  • accessibility.build color generator

  • Adobe Color Wheel

Check Your Contrast



  • Text must stand out from its background

  • Normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1. Large text needs at least 3:1.

  • Test your text and background pair before you commit

  • Contrast checker: siegemedia.com/contrast-ratio

🔤 Choosing Fonts


  • Use Google Fonts: fonts.google.com

  • Pick one font for headings and one for body text. Two is plenty, and one is fine.

  • Choose a readable body font

  • Always add a fallback, like sans-serif or serif

On Google Fonts, copy the embed code from the font's page into the head of your HTML:


Then use it in your CSS:


    body {
        font-family: "Montserrat", sans-serif; 
        font-weight: 400;
        font-style: normal;
        font-size: 16px;
    }

Your Style Kit


Build your style kit, write down:


  • 3 to 5 tone words (warm, bold, calm, playful)

  • Background color, text color, accent color (hex codes)

  • Heading font and body font

💡 Pro Tip


Save your colors as CSS variables so you can change them in one place:


:root {
  --bg: #FFF8F0;     /* background color */
  --text: #2B2B2B;   /* body text */
  --accent: #E4572E;
}

Then use them in your CSS:


body {
  background-color: var(--bg);
  color: var(--text);
}

h1, h2, h3 {
  font-family: "Montserrat", sans-serif;
}

button {
  background-color: var(--accent);
  color: #ffffff;
}

🤝 Accessibility


Why It Matters for Your Portfolio



  • A site everyone can use reaches more people

  • Employers notice care and professionalism

  • Good accessibility is good design, and it helps everyone

Six Quick Checks


  • Structure: Is the heading outline in order and does it make sense?

  • Alt text: Could someone understand the image without seeing it?

  • Contrast: Check each color pair in a contrast checker

Six Quick Checks


  • Keyboard: Is everything reachable with a visible focus outline?

  • Link text: Does the link text make sense out of context?

  • Readable and responsive: Is the text easy to read and does it work on different devices?

Structure

with Semantic HTML


  • Every page needs landmark elements: header, nav, main, and footer

  • Use one h1 per page

  • Headings should follow in order — h1, then h2, then h3, never skipping a level

  • Choose headings for meaning, not size. Use CSS to change how they look.

Contrast and Color



  • Check every text and background pair in your palette

  • Don't use color alone to communicate meaning. Add text or an icon too.

  • Contrast checker: siegemedia.com/contrast-ratio

Accessibility Tools



  • Lighthouse (built into Chrome DevTools)

  • WAVE browser extension: wave.webaim.org

  • Contrast checker: siegemedia.com/contrast-ratio

  • Screen readers: VoiceOver (built into Mac and iPhone) and NVDA (free for Windows)

✅ Take-Home Checklist


  • ☐ One h1, headings in order

  • ☐ Alt text on every meaningful image

  • ☐ Every text color passes contrast

  • ☐ I can Tab through the whole page and see my place

  • ☐ Link text makes sense on its own

  • ☐ Text is readable at 200% zoom and on my phone

🚀 Level Up

With JavaScript


What JavaScript Can Do for a Portfolio



  • A mobile menu toggle

  • A dark mode button

  • A back-to-top button or smooth scrolling

  • Filtering your projects


Keep Motion Tasteful



  • Avoid flashing or auto-playing animation

  • Let people turn motion off. Many people set their device to reduce motion, and your CSS can respect that:
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  * {
    animation: none !important;
    transition: none !important;
  }
}

Bootstrap and Templates


Bootstrap is a CSS framework: includes ready-made, responsive building blocks like navigation bars, cards, grids, and buttons.



getbootstrap.com

Bootstrap Example


  • Add it with one link tag. Copy the current one from the Bootstrap docs: getbootstrap.com


Then add classes to your HTML:

  alt="Recipe Finder home page">
  <div class="card-body">
    <h5 class="card-title">Recipe Finder</h5>

  </div>
</div>

Bootstrap

Trade-offs


Good for:


  • Speed and a polished look

  • Responsive layouts that adapt to tablets and phones

  • Consistent components

Bootstrap

Trade-offs


Watch out for:


  • Sites that all look alike. Make sure to customize your colors and fonts.

  • Learning the class names

  • Accessibility is still your job (alt text, contrast, labels)

🌐 Publishing

With GitHub Pages



What Is Hosting?



  • Your files need to live on a computer that's always on and connected to the internet

  • GitHub Pages hosts static sites for free

  • Static means HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and images, with no server-side code

How GitHub Pages Works


Your files → Your repository → GitHub Pages → A live web address


  • You save changes to your repository (a commit)

  • GitHub publishes the latest version automatically

  • Your site gets its own web address

Where Your Site Lives



  • A repository named username.github.io is published at https://username.github.io

  • Any other repository is published at https://username.github.io/repository-name/

  • On a free account, the repository needs to be public

The Steps at a Glance


  1. Open your repository and go to Settings

  2. Choose Pages

  3. Choose to deploy from a branch: main, and the root folder

  4. Click Save

  5. Wait a minute or two, then visit your address

🔗 Link It Everywhere



  • Add your site to your LinkedIn profile and About section

  • Add it to your GitHub profile

  • Put it at the top of your résumé

  • Add it to your email signature and job applications

  • Add it to your social media bios

Later



  • A custom domain, like yourname.com

  • Other free hosts, like Netlify and Vercel

  • Keep updating your site as you learn and grow
